Mistfall Hunter Tier List: Best Classes for Solo, Duo, and Squad

A practical Mistfall Hunter tier list for solo, duo, and squad play, with class tradeoffs, beginner picks, and a clear non-official ranking method.

Quick verdict: the best class depends on the run

If you need one safe starting point, Mercenary is the most forgiving solo pick in this fan-made Mistfall Hunter tier list. It offers clear frontline pressure and gives a new player more room to recover after a bad trade. Withered Knight is the stronger anchor when a squad needs someone to hold space, while Seer rises when information and safer calls matter more than raw damage.

Shadowstrix and Sorcerer belong near the top of a burst-focused list, but they are not automatic winners. Both ask for cleaner timing, better positioning, and a willingness to accept more punishment when an engagement goes wrong. Blackarrow sits between safety and pressure: its range and scouting value are useful, but it still relies on choosing the right fight.

This is a scenario tier list, not an official ranking and not a claim about a permanent meta. Mistfall Hunter is a live game with balance changes, different team habits, and a large skill gap between players. Read the table as a starting decision, then use the class guide and build planner to turn the recommendation into a run you can actually explain and repeat.

How this class tier list is built

The first dimension is failure tolerance. A class that keeps options open after a missed attack is easier to recommend to a new player than a class that needs a perfect opening. That is why Mercenary can outrank a flashier damage option in solo play even when the damage ceiling is lower.

The second dimension is decision value. Tracking, space control, range, and reliable disengage can win an extraction without appearing in a damage screenshot. Seer and Withered Knight gain weight in duo and squad play because a teammate can act on the information or space they create.

The third dimension is execution demand. Burst and mobility are valuable only when the player can start and end an engagement on purpose. Shadowstrix and Sorcerer therefore move up for experienced groups and down for a first run. The labels describe a use case, not a universal power measurement.

Class-by-class decision guide

Choose Mercenary when your priority is learning the extraction loop without turning every mistake into a lost run. The class gives a readable frontline job: close distance, pressure one target, and leave before the fight becomes too expensive. It is also a good baseline for testing whether your problem is class choice or route discipline.

Choose Withered Knight when your group needs an anchor rather than another damage dealer. Holding a doorway, buying time for a retreat, or protecting a fragile teammate can matter more than topping a damage chart. In duo play, that consistency gives the partner a clearer window to scout, burst, or finish a fight.

Choose Seer or Blackarrow when information is the missing resource. Seer is the better fit for a coordinated squad that can respond to calls, while Blackarrow works for players who want distance and a measured pace. Neither class removes the need to read the map; both reward players who avoid low-value fights.

Choose Shadowstrix or Sorcerer when the group already has a stable plan and you want to convert a clean opening into pressure. Shadowstrix favors flanks and fast disengage. Sorcerer favors zone control and grouped targets. If your team cannot describe who starts, who follows, and who leaves, their theoretical S-tier value is less important than a safer pick.

How to use the list after a patch

Do not move a class between tiers because of one dramatic clip or one early match. First check whether the change affects damage, survivability, range, cooldowns, equipment access, or the way a class enters and exits a fight. A small number change can be less important than a new interaction that changes the whole decision pattern.

Re-test the class in the mode where you intend to play it. A buff that makes solo clearing faster may not improve squad value, while a control or information change may matter most when teammates can coordinate around it. This is why the table separates solo, duo, and squad instead of publishing a single universal S tier.

If two classes are close, choose the role your group lacks and record what happened. That feedback is more useful than chasing a ranking without context. We will update this page when verified patch notes or repeated, explainable gameplay evidence changes the recommendation boundary.

Mistfall Hunter tier list FAQ

What is the best class in Mistfall Hunter?

Mercenary is the safest general starting point for solo players, while Withered Knight and Seer are stronger recommendations when a squad needs an anchor or information. The best class still depends on mode, experience, and the role your team lacks.

What is the best solo class in Mistfall Hunter?

Start with Mercenary if you want the most forgiving frontline plan. Blackarrow or Shadowstrix can be better when you already understand spacing, scouting, and disengage timing.

What is the Mistfall Hunter class tier list for duo play?

Withered Knight, Shadowstrix, Sorcerer, and Seer all rise in duo play for different reasons. Pick the one that complements your partner: anchor, burst, zone pressure, or information.

Is this an official Mistfall Hunter tier list?

No. It is an independent, fan-made comparison based on role clarity, failure tolerance, team utility, and execution demand. Check official notes and your current version before treating a ranking as final.

Should beginners follow the S tier only?

No. A lower-risk A-tier class that you can explain and extract with is usually better than an S-tier class whose timing you do not understand. Use the table to narrow the test, not to skip the learning step.
